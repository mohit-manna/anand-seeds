Cauliflower is a cool-season crop, but the best sowing window depends on your local climate, the variety, and whether you raise seedlings in a nursery or sow directly. Use local agricultural guidance and the seed packet to choose dates for your area; a single calendar recommendation will not suit every region.

## Work back from the expected season

First identify the period when your area is suitable for cauliflower growth and curd development. Then use the variety's stated days to maturity and nursery period to estimate when to sow. These are planning guides, not guarantees: temperature, water, soil, and crop management affect how quickly plants develop.

## Match variety to planting window

Varieties can differ in their suitability for particular seasons and temperature ranges. Choose seed recommended for the conditions your crop will encounter, especially during transplant establishment and curd formation. Ask a local extension service or trusted agricultural adviser about varieties that perform in your district.

## Plan the nursery and field together

Allow enough time to raise sturdy seedlings and prepare the field before transplanting. Avoid keeping seedlings crowded in the nursery for too long, and avoid transplanting into a field that is waterlogged, extremely hot, or otherwise unsuitable. Follow the seed supplier's guidance for seedling age and spacing.

## Consider weather and water access

Check likely temperature patterns and rainfall around both sowing and harvest. Make sure irrigation or another reliable water source is available if rainfall is uncertain. Heavy rain, heat, or cold spells can disrupt establishment and crop development, so have a practical plan for protecting young plants where possible.

## Stagger sowing when appropriate

If you need to spread labor or harvest over time, consider sowing manageable batches at intervals suited to your local season and the varieties being grown. Keep each batch labeled and record sowing, transplanting, and harvest dates. Do not stagger beyond the period when the crop can grow well in your conditions.

## Keep a local planting record

Record variety, seed lot, sowing date, transplant date, weather, and harvest outcome. After a few crops, these notes can help you identify the planting window that works best on your farm. Update the schedule as seasonal conditions change.
