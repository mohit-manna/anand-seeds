Cauliflower needs steady care throughout the season. Check the crop regularly and respond to what the plants and soil are showing, rather than following a rigid schedule that may not fit your field.

## After transplanting

Water seedlings after transplanting and check that they establish without wilting. Replace missing or badly damaged plants early if doing so is practical. Keep young plants free from weed competition and protect them from pests while they are vulnerable.

## Water consistently

Maintain even soil moisture around the root zone, adjusting irrigation for rainfall, soil type, and weather. Avoid both prolonged dryness and waterlogging. Uneven moisture can stress plants and interfere with uniform curd development.

## Manage nutrients and weeds

Use soil-test results and local recommendations to guide fertilizer and soil amendment decisions. Too little nutrition can limit growth, while excess or poorly timed fertilizer can waste money and harm crop performance. Remove weeds before they compete heavily, taking care not to damage shallow roots.

## Scout for pests and disease

Inspect the undersides of leaves, growing points, and the soil around plants. Look for feeding damage, discoloration, wilting, and irregular growth. Identify the problem before choosing a response, and follow local integrated pest-management guidance and all product labels. Rotate crops where possible and remove severely diseased plant material appropriately.

## Protect developing curds

Some cauliflower varieties benefit from blanching, where leaves are brought over the developing curd to protect it from sunlight. Other varieties are self-blanching. Check the variety information and local practice, and avoid tying leaves so tightly that the curd is damaged or airflow is restricted.

## Harvest at the right stage

Harvest when the curd has reached the size and firmness expected for the variety and is still compact. Check the field frequently as maturity approaches because curds can lose quality if left too long. Use clean tools, handle curds gently, and move harvested produce into shade promptly.

Keep a simple field log of irrigation, fertilizer, scouting, and harvest dates. These observations help identify recurring issues and improve care decisions in the next crop.
