Good germination begins before sowing. Seed condition, a clean nursery, even moisture, and suitable temperatures all matter. Use the seed packet and local growing recommendations as your guide, since ideal conditions can vary by variety and season.

## Check seed condition

Use seed from a reliable source and check the lot, packing, and expiry information. Store unopened seed in a cool, dry place as directed on its label. Heat and humidity during storage can reduce viability over time.

If you are unsure about an older lot, do a small germination test before sowing the full quantity. Place a counted sample between clean, damp materials, keep it under suitable conditions, and record how many seeds sprout within the period recommended for the variety. This gives you a practical indication of whether to adjust your sowing plan.

## Prepare a clean nursery

Use clean trays or a well-prepared nursery bed with a fine, free-draining growing medium. Reused containers should be cleaned before use. A medium that holds some moisture but does not stay waterlogged helps reduce seedling stress and damping-off problems.

## Sow evenly and cover lightly

Sow at the depth and spacing recommended on the seed packet. Cover the seed evenly, label the rows or trays, and avoid burying seed too deeply. Uneven sowing makes it harder to keep seedlings uniform and can complicate transplanting.

## Maintain steady moisture and warmth

Moisten the medium before or immediately after sowing, then keep it consistently damp rather than saturated. Gentle watering helps prevent seeds from being displaced. Follow the packet's temperature guidance and protect the nursery from harsh sun, heavy rain, and temperature extremes.

## Care for seedlings after emergence

Once seedlings emerge, provide good light and airflow while protecting them from intense heat. Water when needed without leaving trays standing in water. Remove weak or diseased seedlings and avoid overcrowding so the remaining plants can develop sturdy stems and roots.

## Harden before transplanting

Gradually expose seedlings to outdoor conditions before planting them in the field. This transition, often called hardening off, helps reduce transplant shock. Transplant healthy seedlings when the field and weather are suitable for the chosen variety.

For consistent results, keep notes on seed lot, sowing date, nursery conditions, and emergence. Those records help distinguish seed-quality issues from problems caused by moisture, temperature, or nursery management.
